A pair of Darn Tough socks runs $20 to $30, which sounds steep until you register the purchase and realize the company will replace them free, forever, no receipt needed, the moment they wear through. That's the whole pitch, and it's backed by manufacturing rather than marketing copy.

The Cabot family has run a hosiery mill in Northfield, Vermont since 1978, spending two decades supplying private-label socks to retailers until outsourcing picked off those contracts through the 1990s. In 2004, Ric Cabot bet the opposite direction of everyone else in the industry: instead of following the work overseas, he launched a premium branded sock built on the mill's own manufacturing expertise, backed by an unconditional lifetime guarantee no competitor was offering. He gave the first pairs away at the Vermont City Marathon that year.

Every pair is still knitted, finished, and quality-checked at Cabot Hosiery Mills in Northfield and Waterbury — a fine-gauge Merino wool build, typically a 200-needle count, which packs more loops per inch than a standard sock. That's where the durability actually comes from, guarantee aside. The mill employs hundreds of people in a state that's lost most of its manufacturing base, and Darn Tough holds Berry Amendment contracts to supply the US military — a federal procurement standard, not a marketing claim.

One caveat worth knowing: the Merino wool itself comes from Australia, South Africa, New Zealand, and the American West, not Vermont. Darn Tough is upfront about this, and it falls within the FTC's Made in USA standard, which allows imported raw materials as long as all significant manufacturing happens domestically — knitting, finishing, and packaging all happen in Vermont.

If you're hard on socks — hiking, work, skiing, everyday wear — the guarantee is where the math changes. Longtime owners regularly report five or ten years out of a single pair, which puts the real per-year cost well below whatever you're paying for a three-pack that's shredded by spring.
